The commit history is part of the evidence: a plan committed after the charts is not a plan. Check two things in each repository before its defence: the first commit, and whether the report says that two renders agree. The full brief is labs/capstone/README.md.

How the repository is scored

The rubric

Plan and questions
15%
Analysis and charts
30%
Honesty and uncertainty
20%
Chance checks
15%
Reproducibility and writing
20%
Full marks on honesty go to the report that says what it does not know: intervals named, small groups handled, limits in the reader's words.

Full marks, criterion by criterion. Plan: questions with a unit and a comparison, committed first, exploratory and confirmatory told apart. Analysis: a summary and a chart that suit each variable, with relationships, time and mix examined. Honesty: uncertainty drawn, rates with denominators. Chance checks: the right resampling on the right unit, comparisons counted, a reasoned decision. Reproducibility: one command, two runs that agree, a findings table.

Format

8 minutes for each defence

1 · The reader and the questions

Who the report is for and what it set out to answer. One minute.

2 · Findings, checks, limits

Two or three findings with their charts, how each was checked, what stays open. Four minutes.

3 · The redraw

The assessor names one chart and one change of encoding. You redraw it live and say what a reader would now conclude. Three minutes, with questions.

Peer feedback. After each defence, everyone writes one strength, one question and one suggestion.

Typical redraw requests: bars to points with intervals; colour to small multiples; a count to a rate; a shared scale to free scales, or the reverse; a cut axis to zero. Choose a request whose answer changes what a reader concludes. The redraw shows whether the participant can work with the grammar unaided, which matters most where an assistant wrote the first version.
